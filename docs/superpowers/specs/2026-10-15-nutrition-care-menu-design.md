# Nutrition Care Menu and Intervention Design

## Purpose and boundaries

RND staff need a clear Ingredients → Menu Items → Templates library, a weekly intervention menu that can be created, generated, or loaded from a template, trustworthy nutrient and portion calculations, and reports tied to the correct saved intervention. Food Service keeps its own catalog and calculation domain; its requested changes are to the menu calendar/template user interface. Reuse existing models and flows instead of introducing parallel catalog, recipe, template, or report systems.

This design records the decisions from the planning conversation. `Meal`, `Carb`, and `Snack` are **Menu Item categories**; Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, AM Snack, and PM Snack remain **weekly plan slots**. A Menu Item is backed by the existing clinical `Recipe` model and may contain one or many Ingredients (`FoodItem` records). Keep the current ingredient food-group categories and filters. No Nutrition Care menu slot newly selects an Ingredient or USDA entry directly; preserve old direct-item references so historical plans and templates remain readable until reusable templates are converted.

The Expo mobile app serves Food Service Staff and does not provide the Nutrition Care Food Library, intervention planner, or NCP reports. Its FSS menu, prep, and food-profile screens use the separate Food Service menu-cycle API and models. The planned Food Service calendar/template changes target the web UI; keep the mobile request/response contract and Food Service cost/scaling behavior stable. Since `UnitConverter` is shared, verify any clinical conversion change cannot alter FSS conversion or the mobile profile amounts.

Remove old displayed labels such as Diabetic-Friendly, High Protein, Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner as categories. Keep clinical goals, restrictions, allergies, and nutrition values as separate data; do not convert them into category labels.
- Require at least one measured Ingredient per new Menu Item. Allow a Menu Item with one Ingredient, including fruit Snacks and rice/sweet-potato Carb sides. Multi-ingredient Snacks remain valid. In manual weekly plans and templates, staff may place any Snack Menu Item in any slot, including Breakfast, Lunch, or Dinner; this preserves manually planned patterns such as maternal milk and fruit. Auto-Generate may choose Snack Menu Items only for AM and PM Snack slots. A Carb item is a selected side alongside a Meal in main slots. Carb means a filling side paired with a Meal, including rice or sweet potato; a complete dish containing carbohydrates can still be a Meal.
- Update canonical seed definitions and seeded templates to this taxonomy. Classify existing recipes by what each item is, using explicit canonical mappings plus an inventory of deployed noncanonical rows. `Mixed Vegetable Plate` and `Ginisang Sayote` are Meals, even though their descriptions call them side dishes. Assign a remaining standalone dish to Meal only after checking it is neither a Carb side nor a Snack; do not blindly map a former therapeutic label to a new category. Every active Menu Item must receive one of the three categories. If an existing custom row is genuinely ambiguous, resolve its specific classification before the production data change. Preserve owner-created records and IDs.
- Use a forward, idempotent data migration/backfill and retain migration history. A migration is permanent release history, not a disposable one-time script. If a temporary helper is unavoidable, verify its before/after results, remove it locally and from production after use, and confirm it is absent from the commit and deployed checkout. Avoid seeder-only changes that leave the deployed database with old labels. Do not rewrite historical meal plans, report bytes, or audit revisions.

## Ingredient units and nutrient calculations

- An Ingredient's `serving_size` and `serving_unit` define its nutrient baseline. The Menu Item editor defaults an ingredient row to that unit and offers only directly compatible units: mass ↔ mass, volume ↔ volume, or the exact same count unit. Count ↔ mass, count ↔ volume, and mass ↔ volume require an explicit ingredient-specific measure/density that this scope does not add, so they are unavailable. For example, 0.5 kg against a 100 g baseline means five nutrient baselines; `piece` against g cannot be guessed.
- **Canonical picker units and exact conversion factors:** mass is `mg = 0.001 g`, `g = 1 g`, `kg = 1000 g`, `oz = 28.349523125 g`, `lb = 453.59237 g`; volume is `mL = 1 mL`, `L = 1000 mL`, `tsp = 4.92892 mL`, `tbsp = 14.7868 mL`, `cup = 236.588 mL`; count is `piece`, matching itself only. The Ingredient baseline picker offers these canonical units. A recipe row whose baseline is mass sees the five mass units; volume sees the five volume units; `piece` sees only `piece`. `pc`/`pcs` and `pieces` are legacy spellings for `piece`, not a separate size; they normalize to `piece`. Existing other nonempty count-like baseline units (`slice`, `serving`, `pack`, `unit`, etc.) may use **only that identical normalized unit** until deliberately corrected; do not add them as new baseline choices or infer their size. Accept existing mass/volume word aliases from the current converter (`gram`, `grams`, `milliliter`, `millilitre`, `cc`, `liter`, `litre`, `teaspoon`, `tablespoon`) on input and normalize them to canonical units. Normalize case and whitespace before comparison; show one canonical spelling in the picker. Never apply a cross-family factor. A new `piece` Ingredient must have a measured nutrient baseline for one piece; USDA's food-specific gram weight may support that calculation when available, but never use a generic piece-to-gram factor.
- **Exact factor:** let `q` be the recipe row amount, `Uq` its unit, `S` the Ingredient serving size, and `Us` its serving unit. Convert to baseline amount `B = q × factor(Uq)/factor(Us)` only within the same mass or volume family; for the same count unit `B = q`. The row represents `F = B/S` Ingredient nutrient baselines. For each nutrient key `k` (calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, water, every available micronutrient), `row_k = ingredient_per_baseline_k × F`; `whole_recipe_k = Σ row_k`; `per_recipe_serving_k = whole_recipe_k / recipe.servings`; `planned_item_k = per_recipe_serving_k × consumed_servings`. Allergen membership is inherited from every ingredient, not averaged. An ingredient override replaces `q` for that planned item and reruns these formulas without changing the library recipe. Do not round `B`, `F`, or summed nutrient intermediates.
- **Precision and presentation:** keep quantities positive. Expand `food_items.serving_size` to `DECIMAL(12,6)` and `recipe_ingredients.quantity` to `DECIMAL(16,6)` in forward schema migrations; accept at most six fractional digits on both. The current two-decimal columns cannot retain `1 g → 0.001 kg`. Keep the existing two-decimal `meal_plan_items.quantity` storage and permit consumed recipe servings in `0.01` increments (minimum `0.01`), including fractions below one. Round persisted whole-recipe macro/water totals to two decimals and micronutrients to three only after summing unrounded rows, matching current storage conventions; keep full precision during scoring and scaling. In the PDF, show up to three fractional digits for ingredient amounts and up to two for servings, trimming trailing zeroes; a positive displayed amount smaller than 0.001 shows `<0.001` in its own unit, never `0`. The UI may use more preview digits for values that would otherwise display as zero.
- Follow Food Service's **strict compatible-unit picker** pattern, but calculate nutrients rather than cost. Do not copy Food Service's permissive Non-Auto Grocery typed-unit path. The server rejects cross-family, unequal count, unrecognized new, missing/zero baseline, and invalid quantity inputs. An unchanged nonempty legacy count-like baseline and matching recipe unit may remain usable because their numerical ratio is defined by that same unit. The client preview and server use the same conversion table/rounding contract; the server owns persisted totals. Fix all existing Nutrition Care paths that currently divide raw recipe quantities by the baseline without conversion, including recipe totals, ingredient overrides in meal plans, template snapshots, auto scaling, and report portion data. Do not use the existing converter's default water density for unconfigured mass ↔ volume crossings.
- Use the existing `InfoHint` component beside Ingredient baseline and Menu Item recipe-row unit controls. One concise hint lists all three families and their selectable units, explains same-family conversion, and says count units match only themselves. Do not crowd every ingredient row with repeated prose. Food Service Inventory already has weight/liquid/piece-family hints; add the same family help to its new/edit recipe unit area if absent, keeping its existing Non-Auto Grocery exception accurately described there.
- When an Ingredient baseline unit changes within its family, automatically convert its **serving size** to the new unit and leave the recorded nutrient values and linked recipe row quantities/units untouched. Example: `100 g` becomes `0.1 kg`; a linked `500 g` row stays `500 g` and still represents five baselines. If the size conversion cannot be stored exactly enough to preserve the baseline, block and ask for a deliberate amount/unit correction. When the new baseline family is incompatible with linked recipe rows, block the Ingredient change and show affected Menu Items for explicit correction, following Food Service's correction pattern. Do not auto-convert a linked recipe's stored 500 g to 500 kg or assume piece weight. Historical snapshots remain unchanged.
- Inventory existing ingredient/recipe unit pairs before enforcing the new rules. Correct incompatible active entries with an explicit, evidence-based amount/unit mapping; do not silently reinterpret `piece` as grams or treat a cup as water-density grams. Keep legacy history readable without retroactively changing its nutrient snapshots.
- USDA Foundation/FNDDS/SR nutrient bases are generally per 100 g; branded foods can be per 100 g or 100 mL. Read each imported record's actual nutrient basis instead of assuming all USDA imports are grams: the current importer hardcodes `100 g` and must be checked/corrected for any volume-based branded records. USDA descriptive portions such as cup, slice, or piece can carry a **food-specific gram weight**; do not apply a universal cup-to-gram or piece-to-gram factor. Treat USDA rate-limit responses and transient network failures as retryable: report the failure clearly, save no partial or guessed record, and avoid uncontrolled retries. This needs no new clinical unit family.
- Recipe ingredients represent the **whole batch**, with `servings` as the batch yield. A planned quantity is servings consumed, and its nutrients equal batch nutrients ÷ yield × planned servings. Support fractional consumed servings. Recalculate item snapshots after an allowed ingredient override with the same unit-aware formula. Display precision should not change stored precision or create zero portions by rounding.
- **Approved catalog corrections:** the read-only production/USDA audit confirmed six incorrect source mappings. Preserve Ingredient IDs and use USDA SR Legacy records 168233 for Pork Loin (whole loin, lean only, roasted), 170050 for Tomato (cooked red tomato), 170173 for Coconut Milk (canned), 168156 for Calamansi / Lime Juice (the approved raw-lime proxy), 169711 for Glutinous Rice (cooked), and 171655 for Corn Grits (white, cooked with water, without salt). Replace their source-derived nutrients from the verified per-100-g records. Recompute affected current recipe totals and reusable template nutrients, preserving template identities, serving quantities, and line order. This explicit correction supersedes snapshot preservation only for those affected reusable library templates; historical patient-plan snapshots, archived PDFs, and audit history remain unchanged.
- Resolve the three audited legacy mL/g rows once using USDA's food-specific cup weights and the specified 236.588 mL cup: milk FDC 170872 has 244 g/cup, canned coconut milk FDC 170173 has 226 g/cup, and lime juice FDC 168156 has 242 g/cup. Thus 150 mL milk becomes 154.699309 g, 50 mL coconut milk becomes 47.762355 g, and 15 mL lime juice becomes 15.343128 g. Store these corrected measured gram amounts to six decimals. These are source-specific catalog corrections; they do not introduce runtime cross-family conversions or a generic density.
- For **new** weekly plan/template Menu Item lines, store `unit = serving` and `quantity = consumed servings`; the unit selector is a controlled serving option, not a free-text field. Show any `prepared_portion_amount/unit` (for example, `1 cup per serving`) as a descriptive serving measure, not as an alternate unverified nutrient baseline. When migrating an old reusable template line recorded in the recipe's prepared-portion unit, convert its quantity to servings with `old_quantity / prepared_portion_amount` only when the units are identical or directly compatible within one family. If no defensible prepared portion exists, keep the historical line viewable and require an explicit corrected serving quantity before the template can be loaded for a new plan. Historical plan item units and snapshots are not rewritten.

## Weekly intervention menu

- A patient may have multiple saved Intervention Plans. Each saved Intervention Plan has zero or one weekly Meal Plan, as already enforced in the database. Keep previous interventions and menus readable; editing remains governed by the existing latest-plan rule. **Updated decision, 2026-10-16:** creating a new intervention prefills both the previous prescription/notes and the latest saved menu in that same care cycle. The menu is an editable unsaved draft, so users can change only the necessary parts. Saving the intervention alone does not persist a menu; **Save week** saves an independent menu under the returned new intervention UUID. Never mutate or reuse the previous menu's identity.
- Show the Weekly Meal Plan section and its Create Menu, Load Template, and Auto-Generate paths while creating a new intervention. Explain that saving the intervention is required before the backend can attach its menu, then continue the selected action without losing the draft or forcing the user to hunt for the planner again.
- A new intervention's planner has no saved intervention identity. Read the latest saved menu only as the source for its prefilled draft; do not treat it as the active saved menu. Retain this draft and pending actions after failed intervention validation and after the new intervention is saved. Resolve writes only against the returned new intervention UUID. Canceling back to a saved intervention discards the new draft and must not resume a pending action. If the care cycle has no saved menu, retain the existing empty planner flow. Reuse authoritative saved snapshots and line order; client nutrient values remain untrusted. Legacy lines must remain visible, with unsupported new-use lines requiring correction rather than silent deletion or guessed conversion. Preserve the previous prescription values and notes during prefill; automatic calculation must not immediately overwrite them. Keep the existing explicit goal/calculation controls for intentional changes and the existing first-intervention calculation flow.
- Load Template works like Food Service: select and preview a template as a week calendar, load its baseline items into the editable week, ask before replacing nonempty cells, allow review/edit and Scale to prescription, then Save. Loading into an editable existing weekly plan replaces that plan's menu content after confirmation; it does not create a second plan or alter the intervention prescription. Read-only historical plans cannot be overwritten. Failed load/save leaves the existing plan and draft intact. Preserve template line order, quantities, units, snapshots, ownership, and goal/stage/maternal compatibility warnings. Saving the current week as a template remains available.
- Reuse the existing template-load API for an unsaved preview and the final save. The server resolves source Recipe/template-line UUIDs and snapshots; a draft never supplies authoritative nutrient values. Preview scaling reuses the same algorithm as saved-plan scaling, and preview creates no plan, slot, or item rows.
- Nutrition Care template create/edit/view uses the same weekly calendar and item controls as its intervention planner, rather than a list popup. Unit entry uses compatible choices rather than free text. Keep the template's baseline portions distinct from patient-scaled copies.
- New menu choices use Menu Items, not Ingredients or direct USDA items. Existing historical FoodItem/FDC references continue to render. Reusable old templates containing direct ingredients are converted to one-ingredient Menu Items with equivalent quantities, units, and nutrient snapshots before they are offered for new use.

## Auto-generation

- Replace `Use rice as carb` with **Exclude carbs**. Keep **Exclude snacks**, available for every intervention goal, including liver disease. If snacks are included, fill both AM and PM Snack slots from Snack Menu Items; if excluded, leave both empty and use the existing target redistribution logic. No ready-to-eat Ingredient shortcut supplies new snacks.
- After Auto-Generate is clicked, if carbs are included, require the user to choose exactly one Carb Menu Item from eligible items. Use that **same item** beside the Meal item in every Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner slot across all seven days (21 main slots). Scale its positive quantity per slot with the saved prescription while retaining its identity. Existing carbohydrates inside a Meal do not silently remove the requested side. Exclude carbs removes only the separate Carb side, not carbohydrates inherent in Meal items. A Carb item never fills the Meal position. Auto-Generate never places a Snack in a main-meal position; manual plans and templates may do so.
- Check chosen Carb ingredients against patient allergies before any plan rows are written. If an allergy conflicts, warn with a recovery action and block generation until another Carb is chosen or carbs are excluded. Staff assess the existing free-text dietary restriction notes themselves when choosing a Carb or Exclude carbs; do not add a restriction parser, structured restriction fields, or a confirmation gate. If no usable positive portion can be fitted to the prescription, block and explain the choice; never silently omit, replace, or zero out the chosen Carb. Preserve other existing clinical goal/scoring behavior unless this explicit composition rule changes it. Keep generation atomic: a failure must not leave a partial weekly plan.
- Remove new direct-Ingredient vegetable-side insertion along with the other direct-Ingredient candidate paths. Vegetables already present in a Meal Menu Item remain part of that dish. Do not invent a fourth Menu Item category or a parallel side model.
- Auto-generation continues to use the **selected saved Intervention Plan** and its prescription. Keep the selected Carb identity through any post-generation scaling. Excluded snacks are omitted from the generated week and from the report's snack rows.

## Reports and portion details

- **Nutrition Intervention Plan** (`patient_menu_plan`) is selected per saved Intervention Plan. It uses that plan's prescription and its one linked saved weekly menu, with server-side patient/NCP/plan relationship checks. Standardize current entry points on `intervention_plan_id`; retain only a safe legacy `meal_plan_id` read path for existing saved reports. Never choose an original, first, or latest plan as fallback for this report.
- Freeze each new planned Menu Item's recipe yield, whole-batch ingredient names/amounts/units, per-serving nutrients, and actual planned portion in its snapshot, including after edits/template loads. Fresh report generation must use the saved snapshot, not later edits to the library. Do not claim exact historical recipe quantities for legacy items whose snapshots never recorded them; preserve existing archived PDFs and use a labelled legacy fallback where reconstruction is impossible.
- Freeze each Ingredient's nutrient baseline alongside its original batch row so later planned-ingredient edits use the saved yield and source nutrients. Generation, manual additions, and new library-template lines share the existing Recipe snapshot calculation. A legacy template with no historical ingredient metadata retains its saved nutrients and captures the available current-recipe fallback once at load, with that fallback visibly labelled. Client ingredient previews include water and update the same planned portion metadata shown in the calendar.
- In Portion details, retain the existing overall PDF footprint and three cards per row. Inside each card use compact aligned columns: **Ingredient | Whole recipe (N servings) | Patient portion (X servings)**. The whole-recipe column shows unscaled ingredient amounts; the patient column shows ingredient amount × X/N. Example: 500 g in a five-serving batch, with 1.2 servings prescribed, shows 500 g and 120 g. For an edited plan item, the patient column uses its saved edited ingredient amounts. Single-item Menu Items follow the same rule. Nutrient totals and portion labels must agree. Empty snack rows remain hidden.
- The user specifically requested less vertical space after reviewing the long-label stress PDF (2026-10-16). Give Ingredient names more of each card's width and reduce padding before reducing text size. Check both unusually long labels/many Ingredients and actual seeded catalog Recipes; do not accept three ordinary dishes consuming a whole page merely because the stress case does.
- Legacy saved ingredient edits remain authoritative even when their snapshot lacks recipe yield. Use an explicitly labelled current-yield fallback only when necessary, leave unavailable original batch amounts unavailable, and divide consumed servings by that yield.
- **NCP Summary** stays one report per care cycle. Its intervention section uses the latest saved intervention in that cycle at preparation time. An archived PDF remains the prepared version even after later interventions are saved. Do not turn NCP Summary into one report per intervention.
