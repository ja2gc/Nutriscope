# Nutrition Care Menu and Intervention Design

## Purpose and boundaries

RND staff need a clear Ingredients → Menu Items → Templates library, a weekly intervention menu that can be created, generated, or loaded from a template, trustworthy nutrient and portion calculations, and reports tied to the correct saved intervention. Food Service keeps its own catalog and calculation domain; its requested changes are to the menu calendar/template user interface. Reuse existing models and flows instead of introducing parallel catalog, recipe, template, or report systems.

This design records the decisions from the planning conversation. `Meal`, `Carb`, and `Snack` are **Menu Item categories**; Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, AM Snack, and PM Snack remain **weekly plan slots**. A Menu Item is backed by the existing clinical `Recipe` model and may contain one or many Ingredients (`FoodItem` records). Keep the current ingredient food-group categories and filters. No Nutrition Care menu slot newly selects an Ingredient or USDA entry directly; preserve old direct-item references so historical plans and templates remain readable until reusable templates are converted.

The Expo mobile app serves Food Service Staff and does not provide the Nutrition Care Food Library, intervention planner, or NCP reports. Its FSS menu, prep, and food-profile screens use the separate Food Service menu-cycle API and models. The planned Food Service calendar/template changes target the web UI; keep the mobile request/response contract and Food Service cost/scaling behavior stable. Since `UnitConverter` is shared, verify any clinical conversion change cannot alter FSS conversion or the mobile profile amounts.

## Ingredient units and nutrient calculations

- An Ingredient's `serving_size` and `serving_unit` define its nutrient baseline. The Menu Item editor defaults an ingredient row to that unit and offers only directly compatible units: mass ↔ mass, volume ↔ volume, or the exact same count unit. Count ↔ mass, count ↔ volume, and mass ↔ volume require an explicit ingredient-specific measure/density that this scope does not add, so they are unavailable. For example, 0.5 kg against a 100 g baseline means five nutrient baselines; `piece` against g cannot be guessed.
- **Canonical picker units and exact conversion factors:** mass is `mg = 0.001 g`, `g = 1 g`, `kg = 1000 g`, `oz = 28.349523125 g`, `lb = 453.59237 g`; volume is `mL = 1 mL`, `L = 1000 mL`, `tsp = 4.92892 mL`, `tbsp = 14.7868 mL`, `cup = 236.588 mL`; count is `piece`, matching itself only. The Ingredient baseline picker offers these canonical units. A recipe row whose baseline is mass sees the five mass units; volume sees the five volume units; `piece` sees only `piece`. `pc`/`pcs` and `pieces` are legacy spellings for `piece`, not a separate size; they normalize to `piece`. Existing other nonempty count-like baseline units (`slice`, `serving`, `pack`, `unit`, etc.) may use **only that identical normalized unit** until deliberately corrected; do not add them as new baseline choices or infer their size. Accept existing mass/volume word aliases from the current converter (`gram`, `grams`, `milliliter`, `millilitre`, `cc`, `liter`, `litre`, `teaspoon`, `tablespoon`) on input and normalize them to canonical units. Normalize case and whitespace before comparison; show one canonical spelling in the picker. Never apply a cross-family factor. A new `piece` Ingredient must have a measured nutrient baseline for one piece; USDA's food-specific gram weight may support that calculation when available, but never use a generic piece-to-gram factor.
- **Exact factor:** let `q` be the recipe row amount, `Uq` its unit, `S` the Ingredient serving size, and `Us` its serving unit. Convert to baseline amount `B = q × factor(Uq)/factor(Us)` only within the same mass or volume family; for the same count unit `B = q`. The row represents `F = B/S` Ingredient nutrient baselines. For each nutrient key `k` (calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, water, every available micronutrient), `row_k = ingredient_per_baseline_k × F`; `whole_recipe_k = Σ row_k`; `per_recipe_serving_k = whole_recipe_k / recipe.servings`; `planned_item_k = per_recipe_serving_k × consumed_servings`. Allergen membership is inherited from every ingredient, not averaged. An ingredient override replaces `q` for that planned item and reruns these formulas without changing the library recipe. Do not round `B`, `F`, or summed nutrient intermediates.
- **Precision and presentation:** keep quantities positive. Expand `food_items.serving_size` to `DECIMAL(12,6)` and `recipe_ingredients.quantity` to `DECIMAL(16,6)` in forward schema migrations; accept at most six fractional digits on both. The current two-decimal columns cannot retain `1 g → 0.001 kg`. Keep the existing two-decimal `meal_plan_items.quantity` storage and permit consumed recipe servings in `0.01` increments (minimum `0.01`), including fractions below one. Round persisted whole-recipe macro/water totals to two decimals and micronutrients to three only after summing unrounded rows, matching current storage conventions; keep full precision during scoring and scaling. In the PDF, show at most one fractional digit for ingredient amounts and servings, trimming trailing zeroes; a positive displayed amount smaller than 0.1 shows `<0.1` in its own unit, never `0`. The UI may use more preview digits for values that would otherwise display as zero.
- Follow Food Service's **strict compatible-unit picker** pattern, but calculate nutrients rather than cost. Do not copy Food Service's permissive Non-Auto Grocery typed-unit path. The server rejects cross-family, unequal count, unrecognized new, missing/zero baseline, and invalid quantity inputs. An unchanged nonempty legacy count-like baseline and matching recipe unit may remain usable because their numerical ratio is defined by that same unit. The client preview and server use the same conversion table/rounding contract; the server owns persisted totals. Fix all existing Nutrition Care paths that currently divide raw recipe quantities by the baseline without conversion, including recipe totals, ingredient overrides in meal plans, template snapshots, auto scaling, and report portion data. Do not use the existing converter's default water density for unconfigured mass ↔ volume crossings.
- Use the existing `InfoHint` component beside Ingredient baseline and Menu Item recipe-row unit controls. One concise hint lists all three families and their selectable units, explains same-family conversion, and says count units match only themselves. Do not crowd every ingredient row with repeated prose. Food Service Inventory already has weight/liquid/piece-family hints; add the same family help to its new/edit recipe unit area if absent, keeping its existing Non-Auto Grocery exception accurately described there.
- When an Ingredient baseline unit changes within its family, automatically convert its **serving size** to the new unit and leave the recorded nutrient values and linked recipe row quantities/units untouched. Example: `100 g` becomes `0.1 kg`; a linked `500 g` row stays `500 g` and still represents five baselines. If the size conversion cannot be stored exactly enough to preserve the baseline, block and ask for a deliberate amount/unit correction. When the new baseline family is incompatible with linked recipe rows, block the Ingredient change and show affected Menu Items for explicit correction, following Food Service's correction pattern. Do not auto-convert a linked recipe's stored 500 g to 500 kg or assume piece weight. Historical snapshots remain unchanged.
- Inventory existing ingredient/recipe unit pairs before enforcing the new rules. Correct incompatible active entries with an explicit, evidence-based amount/unit mapping; do not silently reinterpret `piece` as grams or treat a cup as water-density grams. Keep legacy history readable without retroactively changing its nutrient snapshots.
- USDA Foundation/FNDDS/SR nutrient bases are generally per 100 g; branded foods can be per 100 g or 100 mL. Read each imported record's actual nutrient basis instead of assuming all USDA imports are grams: the current importer hardcodes `100 g` and must be checked/corrected for any volume-based branded records. USDA descriptive portions such as cup, slice, or piece can carry a **food-specific gram weight**; do not apply a universal cup-to-gram or piece-to-gram factor. Treat USDA rate-limit responses and transient network failures as retryable: report the failure clearly, save no partial or guessed record, and avoid uncontrolled retries. This needs no new clinical unit family.
- Recipe ingredients represent the **whole batch**, with `servings` as the batch yield. A planned quantity is servings consumed, and its nutrients equal batch nutrients ÷ yield × planned servings. Support fractional consumed servings. Recalculate item snapshots after an allowed ingredient override with the same unit-aware formula. Display precision should not change stored precision or create zero portions by rounding.

Recompute affected current recipe totals and reusable template nutrients, preserving template identities, serving quantities, and line order. This explicit correction supersedes snapshot preservation only for those affected reusable library templates; historical patient-plan snapshots, archived PDFs, and audit history remain unchanged.
- Resolve the three audited legacy mL/g rows once using USDA's food-specific cup weights and the specified 236.588 mL cup: milk FDC 170872 has 244 g/cup, canned coconut milk FDC 170173 has 226 g/cup, and lime juice FDC 168156 has 242 g/cup. Thus 150 mL milk becomes 154.699309 g, 50 mL coconut milk becomes 47.762355 g, and 15 mL lime juice becomes 15.343128 g. Store these corrected measured gram amounts to six decimals. These are source-specific catalog corrections; they do not introduce runtime cross-family conversions or a generic density.
- For **new** weekly plan/template Menu Item lines, store `unit = serving` and `quantity = consumed servings`; the unit selector is a controlled serving option, not a free-text field. Show any `prepared_portion_amount/unit` (for example, `1 cup per serving`) as a descriptive serving measure, not as an alternate unverified nutrient baseline. When migrating an old reusable template line recorded in the recipe's prepared-portion unit, convert its quantity to servings with `old_quantity / prepared_portion_amount` only when the units are identical or directly compatible within one family. If no defensible prepared portion exists, keep the historical line viewable and require an explicit corrected serving quantity before the template can be loaded for a new plan. Historical plan item units and snapshots are not rewritten.
